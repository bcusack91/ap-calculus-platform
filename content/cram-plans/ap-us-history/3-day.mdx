You have **three days** until the AP US History exam. Forget perfection. This plan drills the **9 CED periods**, with priority on periods 3–5 (Revolution through Reconstruction, ~35% of exam content) and periods 6–8 (Industrial Age through Cold War, ~40%). You'll build thesis skills and recognize high-frequency FRQ patterns.

Assume 4–5 focused hours per day. You'll cycle through content review, document recognition, and one timed FRQ per day.

## Day 1: The Foundations — Revolution, Constitution, and Antebellum Crises (4.5 hrs)

These three periods set up every major theme: sectionalism, federal power, expansion, and slavery.

### Period 3 (1754–1800) & Period 4 (1800–1848) review (90 min)
Focus on causation chains — the exam loves asking **why** things changed.

- **Period 3:** Stamp Act crisis → Declaration → Articles of Confederation weaknesses → Constitution as solution. Federalist vs Anti-Federalist split. Hamilton's financial system vs Jefferson's agrarian vision. Marbury v. Madison and judicial review.
- **Period 4:** Louisiana Purchase → Manifest Destiny → Indian Removal (Indian Removal Act 1830). Mexican-American War and sectional tensions. Missouri Compromise, Compromise of 1850, Kansas-Nebraska Act.

> 💡 **Highest leverage:** Framers' tensions (federalism, separation of powers, slavery silence) appear on ~every DBQ. Memorize *why* they compromised vs what they couldn't resolve.

### Period 5 (1844–1877) crisis overview (90 min)
- **Sectional breakdown:** Dred Scott decision, election of Lincoln, Confederate secession.
- **Civil War causes:** Not just slavery, but incompatible economic systems (plantation vs industrial), westward expansion competition, states' rights doctrine.
- **Reconstruction failures:** Presidential Reconstruction (Lincoln/Johnson) vs Congressional Reconstruction. Radical Republicans, 14th Amendment, military districts, black codes, sharecropping trap.

**Key continuity:** Slavery → Reconstruction → Jim Crow. Understand the cause-effect.

### Document recognition drill (90 min)
Study 10 key documents from CED Period 3–4:
- Federalist Papers (Publius on federal power)
- Marbury v. Madison (judicial review precedent)
- Missouri Compromise (geographic solution attempt)
- Calhoun's "South Carolina Exposition" (nullification argument)
- Dred Scott v. Sandford (states rights, citizenship denial)

For each: note the **author, date, historical context, and main argument**. On your DBQ, you'll see these or similar rhetorical moves.

### FRQ warm-up (60 min — no timer yet)
**Prompt:** Evaluate the extent to which the Constitutional Convention of 1787 resolved the major conflicts among the thirteen states.

Write a full thesis and first paragraph (contextualization). Use this template: "The Constitutional Convention resolved X and Y conflicts through [mechanism], but failed to resolve Z, setting up later crises around [theme]."

## Day 2: From Gilded Age Through Great Depression — Industrialization, Reform, Crisis (4.5 hrs)

Period 6–7 contains ~25% of exam points. The exam loves asking about **continuity and change**: How did America industrialize? Who benefited and who lost?

### Period 6 (1865–1898) & Period 7 (1890–1945) review (100 min)

| Period | Key Developments | Causation Chain |
|---|---|---|
| **Period 6** | Industrialization, immigration surge (1880s–1890s), populism | Transcontinental RR → big business → labor unrest → Populist Party → fusion with Democrats → Bryan 1896 |
| **Period 7 Part A** | Progressive Era (1890s–1920) | Muckraking + TR presidency → trust-busting, conservation, labor reforms → New Nationalism vs New Freedom (Taft vs Wilson split) |
| **Period 7 Part B** | WWI + 1920s | US neutrality 1914–16 → Lusitania, unrestricted submarine warfare → US entry 1917 → Red Scare + isolationism 1920s |
| **Period 7 Part C** | Great Depression + New Deal (1929–1939) | Stock crash → bank failures → FDR 1932 → 100 Days → relief/recovery/reform programs → court-packing crisis |

> ⚠️ **FRQ trap:** Progressivism looks unified but splits: TR (strong executive), Taft (constitutional limits), Wilson (tariff/banking reform), socialists (wealth redistribution). Know which Progressive did what.

### Causation framework lock-in (75 min)
Write quick thesis answers (no full essay) to these causation prompts:

1. "To what extent did industrialization drive Progressive reform?"
2. "Why did the US enter World War I?"
3. "How did the Great Depression reshape American federalism?"

**Template:** "X caused Y primarily because [A] and [B], though [alternative cause] also contributed."

### Period 8 bridge (30 min)
Cold War didn't start in 1945 — Roosevelt's alliance with Stalin was strained from day one. Truman, containment, NATO, Korean War, McCarthyism: these set up the modern US role in the world.

### FRQ timed attempt #2 (90 min — full timer)
**Prompt:** Evaluate the extent to which New Deal programs successfully ended the Great Depression.

Full essay, timed. Check [the FRQ practice guide](/courses/ap-us-history/cram/frq-practice) for rubric before you start.

## Day 3: Cold War, Civil Rights, and Modern America — Plus Your First LEQ (4.5 hrs)

Period 8–9 cover America's post-WWII identity and ongoing debates.

### Period 8 (1945–1980) summary (90 min)
- **Cold War shape:** Truman Doctrine, Berlin Blockade, Korean War (containment), Cuban Missile Crisis, Vietnam escalation/withdrawal, detente.
- **Civil Rights causation:** Rosa Parks (1955) → Montgomery Bus Boycott → court victory (Brown v. Board, Gideon v. Wainwright) → March on Washington (1963) → Civil Rights Act (1964) → Voting Rights Act (1965) → King assassination (1968) → conservative backlash (backlash against busing, tax revolt).
- **Great Society:** LBJ's ambition vs Vietnam drain. Medicare, Medicaid, War on Poverty. Unintended consequences.

> 🎯 **Causation that hits every exam:** **Why did Civil Rights legislation pass in 1964–65 but fail to end racial inequality?** Answer: systemic racism, white flight, backlash, de facto segregation (housing, schools, economic access). This is CCOT (continuity and change over time).

### Period 9 (1980–present) highlights (60 min)
- Reagan Revolution: conservative turn, Cold War end, Iran hostage crisis resolution.
- Post-Cold War: Gulf War, 9/11, Afghanistan/Iraq, financial crisis, demographic shift, polarization.

### Top 5 must-know themes across all 9 periods (45 min)

| Theme | Low Period | High Period | Exam Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| **American Identity** | National origins (P1–2) | Multiculturalism, polarization (P9) | 25% |
| **Work, Exchange, Tech** | Mercantilism, plantation (P2) | Deindustrialization, tech inequality (P9) | 20% |
| **Migration & Settlement** | Columbus (P1) | Immigration debate (P9) | 15% |
| **Politics & Power** | Framers' debates (P3) | Executive power post-9/11 (P9) | 25% |
| **America in the World** | Isolation (P3) | Global power, allies (P8–9) | 15% |

### LEQ Practice (120 min — full timed essay)
Choose one of these three:

**Option A (Period 5–6 comparison):** Compare the extent to which Reconstruction and the Gilded Age represented continuity or change in Americans' views on federal authority.

**Option B (Period 8 causation):** Evaluate the extent to which the Cold War, rather than domestic social movements, drove American foreign policy in the 1950s–1970s.

**Option C (Period 9 causation):** To what extent did the Reagan Revolution end the Cold War versus responding to broader economic and cultural shifts?

Write the full essay, timed (40 min). Aim for thesis + contextualization + at least 2 evidence paragraphs. See [the FRQ practice guide](/courses/ap-us-history/cram/frq-practice) for the LEQ rubric.

## Night before: final reset

Skim the [last-minute review checklist](/courses/ap-us-history/cram/last-minute-review). Sleep 8 hours. Tomorrow you'll see periods 1–2 scattered in DBQ documents and maybe one LEQ on them, but the exam is built on periods 3–9. You've drilled the heavyweights.

## What to do if you're behind

- **Short on time?** Skip period 2 details. Focus periods 3–5 (sectionalism), 7 (New Deal), and 8 (Civil Rights/Cold War).
- **Weak on causes?** Do the three causation questions above twice each. Causes = points.
- **Shaky on FRQ?** Spend 2 hours on the practice guide. The rubric is predictable.
