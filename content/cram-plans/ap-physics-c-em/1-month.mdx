You have **four weeks** to build mastery in AP Physics C: Electricity and Magnetism. This plan scaffolds each unit: start with foundational concepts and drills, build to multipart problem solving, and culminate in full FRQs by Week 4.

Daily commitment: **2–3 hours.** Weekends are lighter (review + 1 FRQ).

## Week 1: Electrostatics & Foundations

| Day | Topics | MCQ count | FRQ | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mon** | Coulomb's law, electric field ($E = kq/r^2$), superposition | 20 | None | Algebra & vectors only |
| **Tue** | Continuous distributions: ring, rod, disk ($dE = k \frac{dq}{r^2}$) | 20 | 3 short setups | Know the integral form |
| **Wed** | Electric potential ($V = -\int E \cdot d\ell$), relating $E$ and $V$ | 18 | None | 1D derivative: $E = -dV/dr$ |
| **Thu** | **Gauss's law I:** statement, choosing Gaussian surface, spherical symmetry | 25 | 1 full (sphere) | *This is the unit.* Spend time here. |
| **Fri** | **Gauss's law II:** cylindrical & planar symmetry, inside/outside | 25 | 1 full (cylinder) | Drill symmetry recognition |
| **Sat** | Review Day 1–5 topics, redo 2 hardest FRQs | — | 2 re-worked | Consolidate before Sunday |
| **Sun** | Light review + conceptual Q&A (no new problems) | — | — | Rest & reset |

**Week 1 goal:** Recognize Gauss's law as the foundation. Every exam includes a Gauss's law FRQ.

## Daily workflow (each day)

1. **Review (30–45 min):** Read the textbook section or watch 1–2 Khan Academy videos. Write 2–3 key equations by hand.
2. **Drill MCQ (1 hr):** Timed, no calculator except where allowed. Mark and skip tough ones; come back.
3. **FRQ (30–45 min):** Work fully. Write the integral or derivative *expression*, not just the answer.
4. **Self-check:** Compare to [topic library →](/courses/ap-physics-c-em) or worked examples. Write down your error (e.g., "forgot Lenz's direction" or "used $k$ instead of $\epsilon_0$").

## Common mistakes by unit (watch for these)

| Unit | Trap | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Electrostatics** | Forget to cite *which symmetry* was used for Gauss's law | On FRQs, name the surface (spherical, cylindrical, planar) and explain why symmetry applies |
| **Capacitors** | Capacitors in series: treat like resistors | Capacitors in series have *same* charge, *different* voltage. Use $\frac{1}{C_{tot}} = \sum \frac{1}{C_i}$ |
| **Circuits** | Capacitor conducts current in steady state | No. In steady state, $dQ/dt = 0$, so $I = 0$ through capacitor (open circuit) |
| **Magnetism** | Right-hand-rule sign errors | Practice 3 Lorentz & 3 Ampère problems daily until it's automatic |
| **Induction** | Forget the negative in $\varepsilon = -\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}$ | That negative sign *is* Lenz's law. Always cite Lenz when asked for current direction |
| **RL circuits** | Confuse $\tau = RC$ and $\tau = L/R$ | RC (charge curves), RL (current curves). Write both on your exam reference card. |

## Weekly mock exam guidelines

After **Week 2, 3, 4**, administer a 90-minute mock in exam conditions. Score using the rubric in the [last-minute review →](/courses/ap-physics-c-em/cram/last-minute-review).

- **≥70 points = 5**
- **58–69 = 4**
- **42–57 = 3**
- etc.

If you're at 3 or below after Week 3, spend extra time on FRQ setup and Lenz's law direction (these are 30% of the score and often rushed).
