| Series | Interval |
|---|---|
| $e^x = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{n!}$ | All $x$ |
| $\sin x = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n x^{2n+1}}{(2n+1)!}$ | All $x$ |

## BC essentials: convergence tests

**When you see $\sum a_n$, ask:**

| Test | When to use | Condition | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Geometric** | Form is $\sum ar^n$ | $\|r\| < 1$ | Converges to $\frac{a}{1-r}$ |
| **$p$-series** | Form is $\sum \frac{1}{n^p}$ | $p > 1$ | Converges; $p \leq 1$ diverges |
| **Ratio test** | Factorials, exponentials, powers | $L = \lim \left\|\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right\|$ | $L < 1$ ⟹ converge; $L > 1$ ⟹ diverge |
| **Alternating** | $\sum (-1)^n a_n$, $a_n$ decreasing | $\lim a_n = 0$ | Converges (but test convergence of $\|a_n\|$ separately) |
| **Integral test** | $a_n = f(n)$, $f$ decreasing | $\int_1^{\infty} f(x) \, dx$ converges | Series converges |
| **Comparison** | Compare to known series | $0 \leq a_n \leq b_n$ | If $\sum b_n$ converges, so does $\sum a_n$ |

> **💡 IOC Strategy:** Use ratio test to find radius; then test endpoints *separately* (plug in, use alternating or $p$-test).

## Top 12 exam traps

1. **Polar area:** Write $\frac{1}{2} \int r^2 \, d\theta$, not $\int r^2 \, d\theta$.
2. **Parametric speed:** $\sqrt{(dx/dt)^2 + (dy/dt)^2}$, never $(dx/dt + dy/dt)$ or $|(dx/dt) + (dy/dt)|$.
3. **Parametric $dy/dx$:** $\frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt}$, not $\frac{dy}{dx}$ (which is what you're trying to find).
4. **IOC endpoints:** **Test them separately.** Endpoint convergence can differ from interior convergence.
5. **Ratio test $L = 1$:** Inconclusive — you must use another test.
6. **Lagrange error:** Find $M = \max |f^{(n+1)}|$ on the interval; don't guess a bound.
7. **Chain rule with FTC:** $\frac{d}{dx} \int_a^{g(x)} f(t) \, dt = f(g(x)) \cdot g'(x)$ — don't drop $g'(x)$.
8. **Implicit differentiation:** Every $y$ becomes $\frac{dy}{dx}$ via chain rule; don't forget this step.
9. **Distance vs displacement:** Distance uses $|v(t)|$; displacement is $\int v(t) \, dt$ (can be negative).
10. **MVT hypotheses:** *Continuous on $[a,b]$* AND *differentiable on $(a,b)$* — must state both.
11. **Integration by parts:** Use LIATE priority when choosing $u$. Often $u = \text{polynomial}$ or $u = \ln x$.
12. **Series notation:** Write limits and indices clearly; e.g., $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}$, not just $\sum$.

## Integration by parts: LIATE

Priority for choosing $u$ (so $dv$ is what remains):
1. **L**ogarithm ($\ln x$)
2. **I**nverse trig ($\arcsin x$, $\arctan x$)
3. **A**lgebraic ($x^2$, $x$)
4. **T**rigonometric ($\sin x$, $\cos x$)
5. **E**xponential ($e^x$)

Example: $\int x \sin x \, dx$. Choose $u = x$ (Algebraic) and $dv = \sin x \, dx$.

## Theorems you may cite

- **IVT:** $f$ continuous on $[a,b]$, $k$ between $f(a), f(b)$ ⟹ ∃ $c$ with $f(c) = k$.
- **MVT:** $f$ continuous on $[a,b]$, differentiable on $(a,b)$ ⟹ ∃ $c$ with $f'(c) = \frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}$.
- **EVT:** $f$ continuous on $[a,b]$ ⟹ $f$ has absolute max and min.
- **FTC Part 1:** $\frac{d}{dx} \int_a^x f(t) \, dt = f(x)$.
- **FTC Part 2:** $\int_a^b f'(x) \, dx = f(b) - f(a)$.

## Calculator essentials (check batteries tonight!)

- **Numerical derivative:** `nDeriv(f(x), x, value)`
- **Definite integral:** `fnInt(f(x), x, a, b)`
- **Find intersection:** Graph both functions, `2nd → CALC → intersect`.
- **Find zero:** Graph, `2nd → CALC → zero`.
- **Solve $f(x) = k$:** Use `nSolve(f(x) - k, x, guess)` or graph and intersect.
