## Neurotransmitter Table (Memorize Cold)

| Neurotransmitter | Function | Associated Disorders | Example Drug |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dopamine** | Reward, motivation, motor movement | Schizophrenia (↑), Parkinson's (↓) | Antipsychotics (dopamine blockers); Levodopa (Parkinson's) |
| **Serotonin** | Mood, sleep, appetite, impulse control | Depression (↓), OCD (↓), anxiety (↓) | SSRIs (e.g., Fluoxetine/Prozac) |
| **GABA** | Inhibition, anxiety reduction | Anxiety (↓), epilepsy (↓) | Benzodiazepines (e.g., Valium); Barbiturates |
| **Glutamate** | Excitation, learning, memory formation | Schizophrenia (↑ dysregulation), Alzheimer's (↓) | Ketamine (anesthetic); NMDA blockers |
| **Acetylcholine** | Memory formation, muscle movement, attention | Alzheimer's (↓ in cortex), Myasthenia gravis (↓ at NMJ) | Cholinesterase inhibitors (slow breakdown) |
| **Norepinephrine** | Arousal, attention, alertness | Depression (↓), ADHD (↓), anxiety (↑) | Stimulants (e.g., Adderall); Tricyclic antidepressants |
| **Endorphins** | Pain relief, pleasure, reward | Opioid use disorder (dependence on exogenous opioids) | Naloxone (opioid antagonist); Methadone (maintenance) |

**Key principle:** ↓ = deficiency/hypoactivity. ↑ = excess/hyperactivity.

## Brain Structures (Quick Reference)

| Structure | Location | Function | Clinical Clue |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Amygdala** | Limbic system | Fear, emotion processing, threat detection | Damage → fear extinction; hyperactive → anxiety disorder |
| **Hippocampus** | Limbic system | Memory formation, especially episodic | Damage (H.M.) → anterograde amnesia; hyperactive in PTSD |
| **Cerebellum** | Hindbrain | Motor coordination, balance, procedural learning | Damage → ataxia, clumsy movements |
| **Prefrontal Cortex** | Frontal lobe | Executive function, impulse control, planning | Damage (Phineas Gage) → impulsivity; underdeveloped in adolescence (risk-taking) |
| **Hypothalamus** | Diencephalon | Hunger, thirst, temperature, pituitary control (HPA axis) | Stimulation → feeding/reproduction; damage → anorexia/obesity |
| **Thalamus** | Diencephalon | Sensory relay (except olfaction) | Damage → sensory loss; sleep disruption (thalamus gate-closes in sleep) |
| **Broca's Area** | Frontal lobe | Speech production | Damage → expressive (non-fluent) aphasia |
| **Wernicke's Area** | Temporal lobe | Speech comprehension | Damage → receptive aphasia (fluent but meaningless speech) |
| **Pituitary** | Base of brain | "Master gland"; releases hormones (ACTH, FSH, LH) | Stress response (pituitary releases ACTH → cortisol) |

## Conditioning Vocabulary Cheat Sheet

### Classical Conditioning (Pavlov)

- **UCS (Unconditioned Stimulus):** Naturally triggers response (e.g., food → salivation)
- **UCR (Unconditioned Response):** Natural response to UCS (e.g., salivation)
- **CS (Conditioned Stimulus):** Neutral stimulus paired with UCS; eventually triggers response (e.g., bell)
- **CR (Conditioned Response):** Learned response to CS (e.g., salivation to bell)
- **Acquisition:** Pairing CS + UCS until CR forms.
- **Extinction:** CS presented alone (no UCS) until CR diminishes.
- **Spontaneous Recovery:** CR suddenly reappears after extinction, usually weaker.
- **Generalization:** CR occurs to stimuli similar to CS (e.g., fear of all dogs if conditioned to one).
- **Discrimination:** Learning to distinguish CS from similar stimuli (e.g., only fear this one dog, not all dogs).

### Operant Conditioning (Skinner)

- **Positive Reinforcement:** Add pleasant stimulus → behavior increases (e.g., praise → more effort).
- **Negative Reinforcement:** Remove aversive stimulus → behavior increases (e.g., stop nagging when chores done → more chores).
- **Positive Punishment:** Add aversive stimulus → behavior decreases (e.g., timeout → less misbehavior).
- **Negative Punishment:** Remove pleasant stimulus → behavior decreases (e.g., remove phone → less sneaking out).

**The trap:** Negative reinforcement is NOT punishment. It's *removing* a bad thing to encourage behavior.

## Key Researchers & Studies (Must Know)

| Researcher | Study | Finding | Relevance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pavlov** | Dog + bell + food | Classical conditioning: neutral stimulus paired with UCS triggers CR | Foundation of learning |
| **Skinner** | Operant conditioning chamber | Reinforcement/punishment shapes behavior; schedules affect response rates | Behavioral psychology |
| **Bandura** | Bobo doll experiment | Children imitate aggressive models; modeling is powerful learning mechanism | Observational learning, media effects |
| **Milgram** | Obedience to authority | 65% of participants shocked "learner" on command; situational pressure overrides ethics | Social psychology; Stanford prison analog |
| **Asch** | Line-length conformity | ~35% conformed to obviously wrong group answer; peer pressure real | Group conformity; normative influence |
| **Zimbardo** | Stanford Prison Experiment | Guards became abusive; prisoners became passive in 1 week. Situation matters more than personality. | Situational vs. dispositional attribution |
| **Loftus** | False memory paradigm | Suggestion can create false memories (e.g., "lost in mall" suggestion → false memory of event) | Memory is reconstructive, malleable |
| **Piaget** | Conservation task | Young children fail conservation (fail to understand object permanence); cognitive development in stages | Lifespan development |
| **Erikson** | 8-stage lifespan | Psychosocial crises at each stage; resolution depends on social support | Social development across lifespan |
| **Harlow** | Monkey surrogate mothers | Contact comfort (touch) more important than food for attachment | Attachment, nurture vs. nature |

## Top 10 Traps That Cost Real Points

1. **Confusing negative reinforcement with punishment.** The #1 FRQ mistake. Negative reinforcement *removes* a bad thing. Punishment *adds* a bad thing.
2. **Saying "dopamine = happiness."** Wrong. Dopamine = motivation/reward-seeking. Depression involves serotonin, not dopamine.
3. **Forgetting to name the study/researcher on FRQs.** "Bandura showed..." is worth +1 pt.
4. **Mixing up Broca's and Wernicke's.** Broca's = expressive (speech production, frontal). Wernicke's = receptive (comprehension, temporal).
5. **Confusing proactive and retroactive interference.** Proactive: old learning blocks new. Retroactive: new learning blocks old recall. Memory trick: "pro" = forward in time (old interferes with new).
6. **Assuming short-term memory = working memory.** Close but different. Short-term is older model; working memory is newer (includes manipulation of info).
7. **Diagnosing instead of explaining.** Don't say "This person has schizophrenia." Say "This person exhibits positive symptoms (hallucinations, delusions) associated with dopamine hyperactivity in the mesolimbic pathway."
8. **Forgetting units in real-world FRQ answers.** "The distance is 50" → "The distance is 50 meters" (adds specificity, shows mastery).
9. **Confusing correlation with causation in study design.** Correlation ≠ causation. Only experiments can infer causation (random assignment required).
10. **Rushing the FRQ.** Many students misread the question and answer the wrong thing. Read the FRQ prompt *twice* before writing.

## Refresher: The Two FRQ Types

**FRQ 1 — Article-Based (7 pts):** You read a study summary. Identify IV, DV, design, confounds, apply a concept.

**FRQ 2 — Evidence-Based (7 pts):** You're given a scenario. Use 2+ psychological concepts (define + apply) to address the prompt.

**Structure for both:** Name concept → Define in psychology language → Apply to scenario → Predict/explain outcome.

## During the Exam

**Multiple Choice (60 min, 100 Qs):**
1. Read every choice before selecting.
2. If a question stumps you, **mark and skip**. Do not spend >90 seconds on one Q.
3. Go back to skipped Qs in the last 5 minutes if time permits.
4. Guess on blanks (no penalty for guessing in AP Psych).
5. Watch for trick phrases: "all of the above," "Which is NOT," "best explains."

**Free Response (50 min, 2 FRQs):**
1. **Read all FRQ prompts first.** Decide which you'll tackle first (usually the one you're most confident on).
2. **Budget:** ~25 min per FRQ (including reading + outlining + writing).
3. **Always write the term + definition.** Partial credit rewards clear attempts.
4. **Apply to the scenario explicitly.** Don't assume the grader will connect dots.
5. **Use the study/researcher name if you can.** +1 bonus pt.
6. **Do not leave anything blank** on FRQs. A partial answer earns points; a blank earns zero.
